# Known Limitations

G7 Link aims to be completely transparent about what works out of the box, what requires an optional setup step, and what is restricted by Linux, Android or device hardware.

## Summary of Platform and Hardware Limitations

| Area | Limitation | Why It Happens | Workaround or Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Calls: Two-way voice audio on PC** | You can see caller ID, answer, decline, dial, and (with `Elevated`) mute, hold, toggle speakerphone, and press keypad tones from your PC, but G7 Link's own connection doesn't carry call audio. | Android blocks apps from capturing cellular voice audio (see [`docs/research/hfp.md`](../research/hfp.md)). | Pair the phone with your PC over Bluetooth and click **Connect PC audio** on the call card. This depends on your Bluetooth audio stack supporting the hands-free profile. Otherwise use the **Speaker** button in G7 Link's call bar (with `Elevated`) or Bluetooth headphones paired to your phone. |
| **Clipboard: Automatic background phone-to-PC sync** | At `Standard` and `Assist`, copying text on your phone does not send it to your PC automatically in the background. | Android 10 and newer block background apps from reading the system clipboard unless they run as the foreground app, the active keyboard (IME), or the `shell` user (`UID 2000`). | Enable the **`Elevated`** power level once (Wireless Debugging) for automatic background phone-to-PC clipboard sync, or tap the **Send clipboard** Quick Settings tile, share sheet target, or widget. |
| **Notifications: Sensitive text & OTPs on Android 15+** | At `Standard` and `Assist`, Android 15 and newer may redact notification text containing two-factor authentication codes from third-party notification listeners. | Android 15's Enhanced Confirmation Mode withholds sensitive notification content unless the listener holds the privileged `RECEIVE_SENSITIVE_NOTIFICATIONS` appop. | Enable the **`Elevated`** power level once. G7 Link's shell helper automatically grants `RECEIVE_SENSITIVE_NOTIFICATIONS` to G7 Link. |
| **Messages: Third-party chat app threads** | Unified Conversations from WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, and RCS show messages that arrive while connected and let you reply via `RemoteInput`, but cannot load older historical chat databases or start a brand-new thread with a contact who has not messaged you. | Android sandboxes each third-party chat app's internal SQLite database and does not expose a public API for reading historical third-party messages or RCS databases. | Open the chat app directly on your PC in its own **`app window`** (`Elevated` on Android 11+) when you need to browse old chat history or start a new chat. |
| **Mirroring: `FLAG_SECURE` windows** | Banking apps, password managers, and DRM-protected streaming video apps render black when mirrored at `Standard` or `Assist`. | Android's window compositor strips `FLAG_SECURE` layers from standard `MediaProjection` virtual displays to protect credentials and DRM video. | Normal apps and the Android home screen render normally as soon as you leave the protected window. |
| **Mirroring: `Elevated` `/dev/uhid` and `REMOTE_SUBMIX` on some OEM ROMs** | On some non-AOSP OEM Android ROMs, virtual gamepad registration (`/dev/uhid`) or internal audio capture for apps that block playback capture (`REMOTE_SUBMIX`) may be denied to `shell` (`UID 2000`). | Custom OEM SELinux policies sometimes restrict `/dev/uhid` or `AUDIO_SOURCE_REMOTE_SUBMIX` to `system` (`UID 1000`) or `root` rather than `shell` (`UID 2000`). | G7 Link detects the permission denial automatically and falls back cleanly to standard `InputManager` gamepad injection and standard Android 10+ `AudioPlaybackCapture`. |
| **Webcam: 4K sensor support and 8-bit SDR color** | 4K (`2160p`) webcam streaming requires a physical phone camera sensor and hardware encoder that support 3840x2160 at 30 fps. Video is streamed in 8-bit SDR, not 10-bit HDR. | Emulators and some front-facing phone cameras max out at 1080p. The virtual camera (`v4l2loopback`) takes 8-bit YUYV frames, which is what video calling apps expect. | Use **1080p** or **720p** if your phone's selected camera lens does not support 4K capture. |
| **Wake-on-LAN & BLE Proximity Wake: Hardware dependencies** | Waking a powered-off PC over Wi-Fi rarely works, and Companion Device BLE proximity wake requires a Bluetooth LE radio on both the PC and the phone. | Most PC motherboards cut power to Wi-Fi cards during shutdown (`S5`), supporting Wake-on-LAN only over wired Ethernet or from low-power standby (`S0ix`/`S3`). Android emulators lack a Bluetooth LE controller. | Connect your desktop PC via Ethernet and enable Wake-on-LAN in the BIOS/UEFI and for the network adapter (for example with `ethtool -s <adapter> wol g`). **Settings** shows whether the adapter has it on. |
| **Phone storage: free space on the mount** | `df` and file managers show the mount as 64 GB with 32 GB free, whatever the phone has. | The phone doesn't report its storage size to the PC yet, and a mount has to report something. | Check free space on the phone. Copying still fails normally if the phone runs out of room. |

## Linux Desktop Differences

Linux desktops don't all offer the same things, so some features depend on yours:

- **Clipboard on GNOME with Wayland**: GNOME doesn't give background apps the clipboard, so it syncs only while G7 Link's window has focus. KDE Plasma, compositors built on wlroots, and X11 sessions sync in the background.
- **Global shortcuts on Wayland** (such as the `Command Palette` and `Shelf` shortcuts) go through the desktop's Global Shortcuts portal, so they work only where the desktop offers it. On X11 they work everywhere.
- **Sharing your PC screen** needs X11, or on Wayland the ScreenCast portal with PipeWire.
- **Your device key** is sealed with a key kept in your desktop keyring (GNOME Keyring or KWallet). Without a keyring, it is kept in a file only your user can read, so use full-disk encryption if someone could read your disk. See [Privacy and Security](privacy-and-security.md#1-device-identity-keys).
- **Some features need a package or a permission first**, such as `v4l2loopback` for the phone as webcam and `/dev/uinput` for typing and moving the pointer from the phone. The one-command install sets these up, and the **Connection Doctor** says what's missing.

## Detailed Notes on Key Platform Boundaries

### 1. Android Background Clipboard Access Without `Elevated`

Starting in Android 10, `ClipboardManager.getPrimaryClip()` returns `null` whenever the calling app does not have window focus. There is no runtime permission a user can grant in standard Android Settings to override this for a regular app. G7 Link solves this cleanly at both ends of the spectrum:

- If you stay at `Standard` or `Assist`, you can send your phone's clipboard with one tap from the **Send clipboard** Quick Settings tile (which briefly brings a transparent G7 Link activity to the front to read the clip and closes immediately), the Android share sheet, the home-screen widget, or the app.
- If you enable `Elevated` via Wireless Debugging, G7 Link's local shell helper (`UID 2000`) registers a system clipboard listener through Binder (`IClipboard`) so everything you copy on your phone arrives on your PC automatically in the background, while still honoring Android 13+'s `EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE` flag to never copy passwords.
